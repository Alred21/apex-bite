## What to build

Fit a sculpted, UV-mapped helmeted driver to the refined OTL Superleggero Kart with realistic race gear and true physical joint movement—two-bone inverse-kinematics arms gripping the rotating steering wheel, pedal-tracking boots and legs, and subtle cornering head and torso cues—visible in Chase Cam and Ghost Karts while hiding the helmet cleanly in Helmet Cam.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The driver wears a sculpted Nomex racing suit with natural folds, collar, shoulder pads, a snug rib protector, an anchored four-point racing harness with lap belt and camlock buckle, detailed race gloves, karting boots, and a pearl-white full-face helmet with crimson/gold stripe, vents, eyeport seal, and recessed dark iridium visor with pivot hardware.
- [ ] Left and right arms, elbows, and wrists follow the rotating F1 steering yoke through true two-bone inverse kinematics without tube kinking or clipping against torso, thighs, seat bolsters, or sidepods across full steering lock.
- [ ] Left and right boots, ankles, shins, and knees articulate dynamically via two-bone inverse kinematics to track and depress the live brake and throttle pedals across full pedal travel without boot separation or pedal interpenetration.
- [ ] In Chase Cam and Ghost Karts, the driver's helmet turns subtly toward the corner direction and the torso leans gently with lateral cornering load inside the seat bolsters; in Helmet Cam, the helmet and neck hide cleanly while retaining gloves, sleeves, and clear forward track sightlines.
- [ ] Automated static and kinematic 3D overlap checks confirm zero interpenetration between the driver, seat, headrest, roll hoop, steering column, pedals, wheels, and bodywork across steering and pedal extremes.
